# AI evaluation

Three kinds of AI run in this system. Each is measured against data it was not built from, and each
result below is regenerated by a command, not typed in.

1. **Anomaly detectors** (Phase 7) - seven statistical checks over the mine records, scored against
   the data generator's ground truth.
2. **Predicted risk** (Phase 7) - one trained model, fitted on real US regulator data and transferred
   to these mines.
3. **PPE detection** (Phase 2) - a YOLO model on uploaded images.

None of them decides anything. A finding, a predicted risk or a detected missing helmet is a prompt
for a person to look; the compliance score does not use any of them.

## 1. Anomaly detectors

Seven detectors, stateless functions `payload -> flags`, served by the ai-service
(`POST /anomaly/{name}`, `ai-service/detectors/`) with identical PHP twins (`api/services/detectors/`)
that the API uses when the service is down. Each flag carries its reasons as `{code, params}` - the
numbers that made it fire - and the screens word them in the user's language. Settings (windows,
thresholds, p-value limits) are product settings in `data/schema/rules.yaml` `product.detectors`.

| Detector | What it looks for | Method |
|---|---|---|
| production_anomaly | a day's output far above target, or far from its own 30-day average | above target by more than 50 %; or at least 1.5 x (at most 1/1.5 x) the 30-day mean and 4 standard deviations from it, for the output and for the output-to-target ratio alike (a move the target explains is not flagged) |
| sensor_flatline | a sensor that reads exactly the same value for hours (stuck, covered, bypassed) | 12 h or more of identical readings; the demo fleet's longest innocent flat run is 2 h |
| night_shift | camera-detected violations bunched on the night shift (22:00-06:00 IST) | binomial test of the night share against 8/24, p < 0.001, at least 10 violations |
| repeat_violations | the same category of violation again and again at one mine | 5 or more in 45 days, and either a size-neutral binomial test against the fleet's share of that category (p < 0.00001, about 800 tests per run) or an incident of the matching kind afterwards (roof -> ground movement, gas / fire -> gas, dust or fire, ...) |
| late_actions | corrective actions closed after their deadline, more than chance | at least 10 late, binomial test of the late share against the fleet's, p < 0.001 |
| contractor_outlier | a contractor far outside the others on missing documents or violations per worker | robust z-score (median / MAD) above 3.5, with at least 3 missing documents or 5 violations |
| grievance_cluster | grievances passing their deadline together | at least 5 grievances raised within 10 days at one mine that all breached their deadline |

### How they are scored

`data/out/demo/scenario_expectations.json` lists what the generator planted in the demo data: seven
positives (S1-S7, each must be flagged) and three decoys (N1-N3, look suspicious but are legitimate,
must not be flagged). The detectors run on the demo database at the data's own "now" (the end of the
generated window, 2026-09-25 23:59 UTC), exactly as the scheduled job would. A flag counts as a true
positive when it names the scenario's mine and overlaps its window; every other flag is a false
positive - the generator did not plant it - and is listed by mine.

### The false positives

Every planted scenario is found and every decoy is ignored. The ten other flags are real patterns in
the synthetic data that the generator did not label, not errors of the arithmetic:

- **production_anomaly (4)**: three one-day drops to about 0.65 of the 30-day average on the first
  days of August (the generator's monthly rhythm) and one spike of 1.6 x. Each is a genuine
  deviation; an officer would dismiss them in seconds, which is the intended use.
- **repeat_violations (4)**: four mines with five or more violations of one category followed by an
  incident of the matching kind within 45 days. The generator places violations and incidents
  independently, so these co-occur by chance - but the rule "repeated roof violations, then a roof
  fall" is exactly what an inspector wants surfaced.
- **contractor_outlier (2)**: two contractors with about four times the median violations per
  worker.

Earlier versions produced 27 repeat-violation flags; the size-neutral test and the incident link
brought that to 4 without losing S1.

### Engines

The table is computed with the PHP engine (the fallback). The ai-service gives the same flags: the
parity tests (`ai-service/tests/test_detectors.py` and `api/tests/unit/DetectorParityTest.php`) run
both on the same payloads, dumped from the demo database by `php yii ai/fixtures`, and require
identical output, number for number. `php yii ai/evaluate --engine=ai-service` scores the service
directly when it is running.

## 2. Predicted risk

**Trained on US regulator data and transferred.** No public Indian dataset links mine-level
violations to later accidents, so the model learns that link from the US Mine Safety and Health
Administration's public records and is applied here through the category crosswalk. It is shown
as a ranking signal with its reasons, never as a finding.

- **Data**: `data/reference/msha_rates.csv` - real US coal mine-years (surface and underground, 20 or
  more workers), each with its violations by category, accidents, lost-time accidents and workforce.
- **Target**: a high-accident next year - lost-time and fatal accidents at 3 or more per 100
  workers in the following year (about a third of mine-years).
- **Features** (13, `ai-service/risk/features.py`): workforce size, underground or not, violations
  per 100 workers in total and in seven categories (roof and sides, ventilation and gas, electrical,
  machinery, transport and haulage, fire, PPE), accidents and lost-time accidents per 100 workers,
  and whether there was a lost-time accident this year.
- **Model**: gradient boosting (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, 200 trees of depth 3,
  CPU) with monotonic constraints - more violations or accidents can only raise
  the risk - and Platt calibration. The trees are exported to `ai-service/risk/model.json` and
  evaluated from that file in Python and in PHP (maximum difference from scikit-learn on the test set: 0; the PHP path is the fallback).
- **Split by time**: fitted on 2017-2020, calibrated on 2021, tested on 2022-2024 (feature years;
  each target is the year after). The test years were never seen.

### Applying it to these mines

Each mine's features come from its last 365 days of records (`RiskModelService::features`):
violations mapped to the seven categories by `data/reference/crosswalk_msha_india.csv`, incidents,
and the average daily manpower. Violation rates are not comparable across the two systems - US
inspectors record about 32 per 100 workers a year, our records about 6 - so each violation rate is
placed at the same percentile of the US distribution as it holds in our fleet (a mine with none
stays at none). Accident rates are used as they are.

On the demo fleet (after `yii jobs/score`): 62 mines low (< 25 %), 9 medium, 3 high; median 4.8 %.
Each prediction lists up to three factors, each with how many percentage points it adds compared
with a typical mine, and where the mine stands in the fleet on it.

**What it is not.** It is not calibrated for India - mines here differ in size, methods and
reporting - and the demo data is synthetic, so it cannot be validated on these mines. It is shown
beside the Governance Risk Index, with this statement on screen, and does not change the compliance
score or the inspection order.

## 3. PPE detection

The ai-service (`ai-service/`) detects PPE in an uploaded image with a YOLO model and the
PPE rules (`ai-service/vision/ppe_rules.py`, from the prototype): a person detected without the
required PPE (helmet and vest by default, `REQUIRED_PPE`) becomes a violation. This page records
where the model comes from, its licences and how well it does on held-out data.

### Model choice (2026-09-27)

First choice was an openly licensed, downloadable pretrained PPE model. Candidates checked:

| Candidate | Why not used |
|---|---|
| `keremberke/yolov8n-hard-hat-detection` (Hugging Face) | No licence stated in the model card or metadata - no right to use or redistribute. |
| `Hansung-Cho/yolov8-ppe-detection` (Hugging Face) | Labelled MIT, but trained on "public PPE / construction-related datasets (Kaggle etc.)" whose licence "follows the data provider"; the training data cannot be identified, so the licence chain cannot be recorded. |
| SH17 release weights | The SH17 images need a Kaggle account (data/SOURCES.md, S13); not downloadable without login. |

So the model is **fine-tuned here on a dataset with a known licence**, reproducibly, by
`scripts/build_ppe_model.py`:

| Part | What | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Base weights | Ultralytics YOLO11n, COCO-pretrained (`yolo11n.pt`, Ultralytics GitHub release assets, no login) | AGPL-3.0 (see below) |
| Training data | S13: Roboflow `ppe-detection-ozhfb` v14 (workspace `sdp-lfigk`), mirrored in `vyasdeepti/PPE-Object-Detection-using-YOLO11` at commit `98085c8a` - 6 classes: Gloves, Hard_hat, Mask, Person, Safety_boots, Vest | CC BY 4.0 (dataset's own `README.dataset.txt` and `data.yaml`) - attribution required |
| Splits | the export's own: 1,101 train / 305 valid / 213 **test** images. The test split is used only for the final numbers below. | - |
| Output | `ai-service/ml/weights/ppe.pt` (gitignored; rebuild with the script) | AGPL-3.0 per Ultralytics (trained with its code) |

Attribution for the data: "PPE Detection" dataset by Roboflow Universe user sdp-lfigk
(https://universe.roboflow.com/sdp-lfigk/ppe-detection-ozhfb/dataset/14), CC BY 4.0.

### Ultralytics and AGPL-3.0 - what it means for this project

`ultralytics` (the library the prototype already used, and the base weights) is licensed under
the **GNU Affero General Public License v3**, including its section 13 (remote network
interaction). Ultralytics states on https://www.ultralytics.com/license that "All Ultralytics YOLO
trained models fall under the AGPL-3.0 License by default", and that compliance means publicly
releasing "the complete corresponding source code for the entire derivative work, including the
larger application ... and, where applicable, model weights" - or buying an Enterprise License,
which it names for "SaaS platforms, APIs, or cloud systems that use YOLO behind the scenes".

For CoalShield that means:

- As a hackathon prototype whose source is shared, running YOLO in `ai-service/` is compatible
  with AGPL-3.0 **if** the project's source (including the ai-service, its scripts and the trained
  weights) is published under AGPL-3.0-compatible terms whenever the service is offered to users
  over a network.
- A closed-source or commercial deployment (for example a government or company rollout that is
  not open-sourced) would need an **Ultralytics Enterprise License**, or the detector would have to
  be replaced by a permissively licensed one. The ai-service boundary (HTTP, one endpoint) keeps
  such a swap contained to `ai-service/`.
- This is a licensing decision for the project owner, not a legal opinion.

**Owner decision (2026-09-27):** the repository will be **public and open-source for SIH**, which
satisfies AGPL-3.0 for this use. The source - including `ai-service/`, the training script and the
selection script - is in the repository. The trained weights are gitignored for size but rebuilt
from public inputs by `scripts/build_ppe_model.py`. Whenever the service is offered to others over
a network, the conservative course is also to publish the exact weights in use (for example as a
release asset next to the source), since Ultralytics names model weights as part of the
corresponding source.

**A closed deployment** (a government or company rollout that is not open-sourced) **would need a
licence review first**: an Ultralytics Enterprise License, or a detector under a permissive
licence. The ai-service boundary keeps such a swap contained to `ai-service/`.

### Rebuilding the weights

```bat
ai-service\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\build_ppe_model.py
```

The defaults reproduce the installed model (run 2 below): 25 epochs at 640 px, nothing frozen,
three warm-up epochs - about **2 hours on the reference CPU** (no GPU). It writes
`ai-service/ml/weights/ppe.pt` and the results section at the end of this page. The quicker first run
is `--epochs 10 --imgsz 512 --freeze 10 --warmup 1` (about 20 minutes); `--candidate --name <run>`
trains and tests without installing. `run_all.bat` warns if the weights are missing; the ai-service
then falls back to its test fixture (sidecar files next to the sample images), which exists for the
automated tests (`PPE_DETECTOR=fixture`) and is never used in the demo.

### Two runs, one kept (2026-09-27)

Both runs start from YOLO11n (COCO) with seed 2026 and were evaluated on the same held-out test
split (213 images), never used for training or checkpoint selection. The kept model is the one
that scores better **on that split**.

| Precision / recall (all) | 0.777 / 0.848 | **0.818 / 0.889** |
| mAP50 (all) | 0.850 | **0.878** |

### Demo images (held-out only)

The demo uses **only held-out test images**, copied unmodified (CC BY 4.0, attributed) to
`ai-service/samples/heldout/` by `scripts/select_ppe_demo_images.py`: three clean frames and three
with violations, chosen from the images where the model's violation set equals the ground truth's,

the folder's README says so. Through the product (`POST /v1/vision/analyze`, mine head of Jayant on

`heldout_01_clean.jpg` → a clean frame that resolves the open PPE findings from vision.

### Limits - read before a demo

- **In-distribution numbers.** The test split comes from the same Roboflow project as the training
  data (construction-style photos). The numbers say how well the model does on *that kind* of
  image. Nothing here is coal-mine footage (lighting, dust, cap lamps, underground scenes), so
  performance at a mine is **not measured**.
- **Mask** is the weak class (mAP50 0.62); masks are not in the default `REQUIRED_PPE` (helmet, vest).
- **The repository's sample photos** (`ai-service/samples/images/`, not part of S13), confidence
  0.45, 2026-09-27:

  | Image | What it shows | Run 1 (512 px) | Run 2 (640 px) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `with_ppe.jpg` | two workers with hard hats and vests | 2 vests, 1 person, no hard hat: false `no_helmet` | 1 hard hat, 2 vests, no person: "clean" for the wrong reason |
  | `demolition_site_workers.jpg` | same photograph | false `no_helmet` | 1 hard hat, 2 vests, no person |
  | `metro_shaft_workers.jpg` | group in a shaft, hard hats | 1 hard hat, no person | nothing detected: "no workers seen" |
  | `derailment_inspector.jpg` | inspector with hard hat and vest | clean frame - correct | clean frame - correct |
  | `ppe_sample.jpg` | synthetic drawing (fixture only) | 2 hard hats | 2 hard hats - not meaningful |

  Run 2 no longer raises the false `no_helmet`, but on photos unlike its training set it still
  **misses people**, and without a person no violation can be inferred. That is why the demo uses
  held-out test images, and why a clean frame counts as evidence only when it shows a person or
  worn PPE (`EVIDENCE_LABELS`). A model for mine use needs mine footage to train and test on.

### How the detections are used

Only `Hard_hat` (→ helmet), `Vest`, `Person`, and optionally `Mask`, `Gloves` and `Safety_boots`
matter to the rules. The model has no "no helmet" class; a violation is inferred when a detected
person has no helmet or vest box inside it (`ppe_rules.py`, containment threshold 0.5). So person
and hard-hat recall drive how many violations are caught, and hard-hat and vest precision drive
false alarms.
